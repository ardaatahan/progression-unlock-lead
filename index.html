Step 1 — Pick 3 lifts

One from each pattern. Pick lifts you already do and can repeat every week.

Step 2 — Set a rep range for each

Pick a range about 4 reps wide. Common choices: 5–8, 6–10, or 8–12.

Step 3 — The double-progression rules

  1. Use the same load every session until you hit the top of the range on every work set with clean form.
  2. Then add load: the smallest jump available (about 2.5–5 lb upper body, 5–10 lb lower body), and drop back to the bottom of the range.
  3. End most work sets with about 0–3 reps in reserve (RIR). RIR means how many more clean reps you could have done.
  4. Progress means more load or more clean reps than last time. Pump and soreness don't count.

Step 5 — The 60-second stall check

For each lift, look at your last sessions:

  • Did you add load or add at least one clean rep in the last 2 sessions? → Not stalled. Keep going.
  • 2 or more sessions at the same load without hitting the top of the range clean? → Stalled.

Stalled? Here's the part this card can't do

A stall has three possible causes: effort (sets ending too easy), volume (too few or too many hard sets), or recovery (sleep, stress, fatigue piling up). Each needs a different fix, and guessing wrong costs you weeks.
